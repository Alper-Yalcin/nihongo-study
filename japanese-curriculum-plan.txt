# Japanese Learning Platform — Full Curriculum Blueprint

## Context

The app (kototsuna) already has a solid kana system (hiragana/katakana tracks with typing, drawing, listening modes and SRS review). The goal is to expand it into a complete Japanese learning platform by adding a **"Learn Japanese" track** that takes users from zero to JLPT N5/N4.

The existing `learnCourseContent.ts` has a loose 8-unit structure (`LearnCourseUnit` interface) but lacks granular step/question data. The existing roadmap types (`StudyTrackDefinition` → `StudyLevelDefinition` → `StudyLessonDefinition`) are kana-specific. The new track needs its own parallel data architecture.

---

## Engagement Design System (REFINEMENT — added on top of original blueprint)

### Philosophy: Steps Are MISSIONS

Every step is a **mission** — a small, real-life task the learner must accomplish. This shifts the experience from "studying vocabulary" to "doing something in Japanese."

**Mission anatomy (maps to `JLearnStep`):**
```
MISSION
├── scenario        // Real-life setup ("It's 8 AM. You see your teacher in the hallway.")
├── goal            // Single clear task ("Greet your teacher correctly.")
├── vocabulary      // Only words needed to complete this mission (5–12)
├── grammar         // 1–2 patterns, explained in plain English (no jargon)
├── examples        // 3–5 model answers showing the pattern in context
├── tasks           // Heavily active — sentence-build + dialogue-complete prioritized
└── successMessage  // What the user just unlocked ("You can now greet anyone in Japanese!")
```

`successMessage` maps to a new optional field on `JLearnStep` — add it when implementing. It is the ONLY narrative copy shown at mission completion.

---

### Grammar Explanation Rules (non-negotiable)

No linguistic terminology. Explain what it DOES, not what it IS. One sentence max.

| BAD (jargon) | GOOD (plain English) |
|---|---|
| "〜は is a topic marker" | "Put は after the thing you want to talk about." |
| "〜です is a copula forming a nominal predicate" | "Add です at the end to finish the sentence politely." |
| "から is a case particle indicating origin" | "Put から after where you came from." |
| "〜ます is a polite verb conjugation" | "Verbs ending in ます sound polite and complete." |

---

### Task Type Priority (per mission)

| Type | Priority | Rule |
|------|----------|------|
| `sentence-build` | **HIGH** | Every mission needs at least 2 |
| `dialogue-complete` | **HIGH** | At least 1 per mission — real interaction feel |
| `context-select` | **HIGH** | Situation-based choice — tests judgment, not recall |
| `translation-en-jp` | MEDIUM | Active production, max 1–2 per mission |
| `fill-in-blank` | MEDIUM | Good for grammar reinforcement |
| `listening-recognition` | MEDIUM | At least 1 per mission where audio exists |
| `multiple-choice` | LOW | Max 1 per mission — avoid passive recognition overload |

---

### Mandatory Unit Flow Pattern

Every unit follows this exact 5-mission structure:

```
Mission 1: INTRODUCE   — new vocab + 1 grammar, scenario-driven, guided
Mission 2: EXPAND      — new vocab builds on M1, same grammar in new context
Mission 3: REVIEW      — no new vocab, reinforces M1+M2, different scenarios, no hints
Mission 4: PRODUCE     — full dialogue/output mission, no hints, high sentence-build ratio
Mission 5: CHECKPOINT  — tests all unit content, 10–12 questions, 80% pass required
```

---

### Success Message Copy Template

| Mission | Message pattern |
|---------|----------------|
| M1 (Introduce) | "You can now [do X] in Japanese!" |
| M2 (Expand) | "You know [X] — and when to use it!" |
| M3 (Review) | "Your [topic] is getting stronger!" |
| M4 (Produce) | "You just had a real Japanese conversation!" |
| M5 (Checkpoint pass) | "[Unit/Level] complete! You unlocked [next topic]." |

---

### Learning Flow Guidance

The user always follows this path — no branching, no overwhelm:

```
Level unlocked
  └─ Unit 1
       └─ Mission 1 (guided — new vocab shown before tasks)
       └─ Mission 2 (semi-guided — vocab available on tap)
       └─ Mission 3 (review — no hints, no new content)
       └─ Mission 4 (produce — dialogue output, no vocab panel)
       └─ Mission 5 (checkpoint — locked until M1–M4 complete, 80% to pass)
  └─ Unit 2 (unlocked only after Unit 1 checkpoint passes)
  └─ Level Checkpoint (unlocked after all units pass)
```

The learner never skips or reorders missions. The path is linear within a unit. Between levels, the world map (Duolingo-style path) shows progress visually.

## Immersion & Memory Design System (UPGRADE)

### User Identity (constant across ALL levels)

All missions address the learner as a single consistent character:

> **You are an exchange student from your home country, spending one year at a Japanese high school in Tokyo. You live with the Tanaka host family. You spoke zero Japanese when you arrived.**

This identity never changes. Every scenario is filtered through it:
- Level 1: First night at the Tanaka home + first walk to school
- Level 2: First day in homeroom class, meeting classmates
- Level 3: Planning your week, shopping at the local market
- Level 4–7: Deepening into school life, routines, neighborhood

The learner is never "doing drills." They are living through a year in Japan.

---

### Narrative Arc Design

Each unit is a **mini-story** with a clear arc. Missions flow like scenes.

**Unit story structure:**
```
Mission 1 (INTRODUCE): Establish the situation — enter the world, first contact
Mission 2 (EXPAND):    Complication — meet someone new or deepen the interaction
Mission 3 (REVIEW):    Same world, different moment, no hints
Mission 4 (PRODUCE):   Climax — full interaction, no scaffolding, real output
Mission 5 (CHECKPOINT): Resolution — prove you can do it completely alone
```

**World map — level-to-location mapping:**
| Level | Location | Narrative moment |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| L1 — Greetings | Tanaka family home + school gate | First night + first morning |
| L2 — Self Introduction | Homeroom classroom + school lunch | First day meeting people |
| L3 — Numbers & Time | Local market + café | Making plans with new friends |
| L4 — Demonstratives | Convenience store + shopping | "What's that?" moments |
| L5 — Possession | School hallway + lost-and-found | Whose bag is this? |
| L6 — Location & Existence | Neighborhood streets | Asking for directions |
| L7 — Basic Verbs | Tanaka home + morning routine | Daily life with host family |
| L8 — Daily Routines | School schedule + weekends | Scheduling your week |

Each level is a milestone in the exchange student story.

---

### Dialogue Chain Design

Every mission includes at least one **dialogue chain** — a multi-turn interaction where the learner produces responses to a live conversation. Single-question tasks are NOT enough for conversational fluency.

**Format:**
```
Turn 1: [Person A]: Japanese prompt (displayed — not produced by learner)
        [You]: ___ ← Learner produces (sentence-build or fill-in-blank)
Turn 2: [Person A]: Contextual follow-up (pre-written response)
        [You]: ___ ← Learner produces again
Turn 3: (optional) [Person A]: Final line
        [You]: ___ ← Learner completes the exchange
```

**Rules:**
- Minimum 2 turns per chain
- A's Turn 2 response is fixed (pre-written, does not adapt)
- Wrong answer shows the correct answer, then advances to next turn
- All vocabulary in the chain comes from current or prior missions only
- Each chain ends with a natural conversation close (goodbye, thank you, etc.)

**Example chain (Level 1, Mission 1):**
```
[Tanaka-sensei]: おはようございます！
[You]: ___                            → おはようございます！
[Tanaka-sensei]: きょうもがんばりましょう！
[You]: ___                            → はい！よろしくおねがいします！
```

### Spaced Repetition System Design

Vocabulary progress is tracked per item. This mirrors the existing `KanaProgress` pattern from `kanaProgress.ts` — applied to `VocabItem`.

```typescript
// Conceptual model — implement parallel to KanaProgress
interface VocabProgress {
  vocabId: string;
  masteryLevel: 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4; // 0=new → 4=mastered
  errorCount: number;                 // Total errors across all missions
  lastSeenAt: string;                 // ISO timestamp
  nextReviewAt: string;               // SRS scheduled review
  seenInMissions: string[];          // Which mission IDs this appeared in
  confusedWith?: string[];           // Frequently co-confused vocab IDs
}
```

**Review intervals by mastery level:**
| Mastery | Status | Next review after |
|---------|--------|------------------|
| 0 | New | Next session warm-up |
| 1 | Seen once | +1 day |
| 2 | Correct twice | +3 days |
| 3 | Known | +7 days |
| 4 | Mastered | +30 days |

**How content reappears:**
- Mastery 0–1 items → Session warm-up (3–5 quick questions at session start)
- `errorCount > 2` → Item flagged "weak," inserted into next review mission
- `errorCount > 5` → Dedicated "Weak Items" practice session unlocked
- Every M3 (Review mission) mixes current vocab with weak items from prior units
- Grammar weak spots: 3+ failures on same pattern → extra fill-in-blank tasks appear in next 2 missions

### Progression & Reward System

**Unlock chain:**
```
Pass Mission 5 checkpoint (80%+) → Next Unit unlocks
Pass all Unit checkpoints in Level → Level Checkpoint unlocks
Pass Level Checkpoint (80%+) → Next Level unlocks + Badge awarded
```

**Badges (capability, not score):**
| Earned at | Badge | What the learner can now do |
|-----------|-------|----------------------------|
| L1 complete | Social Starter | Handle everyday social moments in Japan |
| L2 complete | Self-Introducer | Introduce yourself fully to anyone in Japanese |
| L3 complete | Time Keeper | Discuss time, dates, and numbers in conversation |
| L6 complete | Navigator | Ask for directions and find things around town |
| L7 complete | Daily Speaker | Describe what you do every day in Japanese |
| L14 complete | N5 Ready | Covered all JLPT N5 core content |

**Milestone messages (shown at level completion, after the badge):**
- L1: "You've landed in Japan — socially."
- L2: "Your classmates know who you are."
- L3: "You can make plans, read timetables, and count anything."
- L14: "JLPT N5 — you're ready."

**World map visual rules:**
- Completed levels: glowing, labeled with badge
- Current level: pulsing animation, shows unit progress dots
- Locked levels: greyed, lock icon visible — no peeking at content
- The path is a literal route through Japan: airport → school → market → station → etc.

### Global Learning Loop

**What happens when the user opens the app:**

```
APP OPEN
  ├─ Active mission pending? → "Continue" (resume exactly where they left off)
  ├─ SRS reviews due?        → "X words to review" soft banner (not forced)
  └─ All caught up?          → "Start next mission" + "Daily challenge" suggestion

SESSION (per visit):
  Step 1 — WARM-UP (60 sec)
    → 3–5 SRS review questions (context-select or listening, fast)
    → Feeds mastery scores silently
  Step 2 — MISSION (1–2 min)
    → Full mission: scenario → dialogue chain → tasks
  Step 3 — COOL-DOWN (10 sec)
    → successMessage displayed
    → Streak updated
    → "Next mission" or "Done for today" choice

WEAK ITEM LOOP (background, between sessions):
  After each mission:
    1. errorCount updated for all vocab seen
    2. masteryLevel recalculated
    3. nextReviewAt scheduled per SRS intervals
    4. Weak items (errorCount > 2) queued for next warm-up
  If 3+ days missed:
    → Gentle re-entry: "Welcome back! Let's refresh."
    → 10-question review of most-recently-seen vocab before new missions
```

### Branching Decision & Emotional Feedback System

Every mission presents a **branching choice** — 3 options, each with a distinct outcome. This is the core mechanic that makes the system feel like a real experience, not a quiz.

**Branching structure:**
```
BRANCHING CHOICE
├── Option A: Wrong — specific character reaction showing the error
├── Option B: Partial — character acknowledges but shows gap or awkwardness
└── Option C: Correct — character reacts positively and continues the interaction
```

**Three outcome types:**
| Outcome | Character reaction | System response |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|
| Wrong | Character pauses, looks confused, waits | Hint shown → retry |
| Partial | Character responds but something feels off | Explanation shown → continues |
| Correct | Character reacts naturally and warmly | Dialogue chain advances |

**Character personalities (how they react differently):**
| Character | To wrong | To partial | To correct |
|-----------|----------|------------|------------|
| Mrs. Tanaka | Pauses, waits patiently | Smiles but seems to expect more | Beams warmly |
| Yuki | Tilts head, giggles | Laughs a little | Exclaims enthusiastically |
| Tanaka-sensei | Quiet pause, stern look | Nods but keeps a neutral expression | Gives a firm approving nod |
| Yamada-sensei | Repeats the question slowly | Gives a polite correction | Smiles and continues |
| Keita | Acts confused | Shrugs and moves on | Laughs and responds casually |
| Sora | Goes quiet | Nods slowly | Smiles shyly |

**Emotional feedback (after each mission completes):**
Each mission ends with a one-line emotional narrative — NOT a score or "great job" — describing how the moment felt:
- "Mrs. Tanaka looks relieved — you handled your first moment in Japan."
- "Tanaka-sensei gives a firm nod of approval."
- "Yuki grabs your arm excitedly. You've made a friend."
- "The class goes quiet — then someone claps. Others follow."

This maps to `successMessage` on `JLearnStep`. The copy must name a feeling AND a capability.

## Final Polish Layers — Living World System (FINAL UPGRADE)

These eight layers sit ON TOP of everything above. They do not replace missions, branching, or dialogue chains — they make the system *react*. Together they turn a structured curriculum into a responsive world that remembers the learner, adapts to them, and pulls them back in.

---

### Layer 1 — Adaptive User State (ENGINE)

The system carries a live model of the learner. Every mission reads it. Every character reacts to it.

```typescript
// Conceptual model — implement alongside VocabProgress
interface UserState {
  confidenceLevel: 'low' | 'medium' | 'high';     // Derived from recent accuracy
  recentMistakes: string[];                       // Last ~10 vocab/grammar IDs that failed
  relationshipLevels: Record<CharacterId, 0|1|2|3>; // 0=stranger, 1=acquaintance, 2=friend, 3=close
  formalityPreference: 'casual' | 'neutral' | 'polite'; // Inferred from branching choices
  missedDays: number;                              // Days since last session
  streakDays: number;
}
```

**How the state updates:**
| Trigger | Effect |
|---------|--------|
| Mission passed first try, 100% | confidenceLevel +1 toward `high` |
| Mission retried 3+ times | confidenceLevel -1 toward `low` |
| Character interacted with | relationshipLevels[characterId] ticks up after 3 missions together |
| Learner repeatedly picks casual option in branching | formalityPreference drifts toward `casual` |
| Wrong answer on same vocab/grammar | Added to recentMistakes (FIFO, cap 10) |

**How the state affects the experience:**

| State | Dialogue effect | Difficulty effect |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| `confidenceLevel: low` | Characters speak slower, repeat more, add encouraging lines ("ゆっくりでいいよ") | Hints auto-appear after 1 wrong answer; vocab panel stays visible longer |
| `confidenceLevel: high` | Characters drop into casual speech, skip slow setup, throw idioms | No hints shown; tasks may include preview vocab from next unit |
| `relationshipLevels[yuki] >= 2` | Yuki uses あなた → きみ, drops honorifics, teases you | Yuki's turns in dialogue chains become more colloquial |
| `formalityPreference: casual` | More casual branch options offered first | Review missions over-sample casual-register items |
| `missedDays >= 3` | Mrs. Tanaka greeting: 「おかえり！さびしかったよ」 | Warm-up mission auto-inserted using recentMistakes |

The state is invisible. The learner never sees it — they just feel the world noticing them.

---

### Layer 2 — Fail-Forward System

Wrong answers don't always mean "retry." Some mistakes make the conversation continue — awkwardly, realistically — and the learner recovers later.

**Three failure modes per mission:**

| Mode | When to use | What happens |
|------|------------|-------------|
| **Retry** | Core grammar/vocab error that breaks comprehension | Hint shown, learner tries again (existing behavior) |
| **Fail-forward** | Socially awkward but understandable — wrong register, wrong honorific, missed cultural phrase | Character reacts with mild confusion/amusement, conversation continues, correct form shown in their reply |
| **Recovery window** | Fail-forward moment flagged for later | The same vocab/grammar reappears in this mission's OR the next mission's dialogue chain — learner gets a second chance without being told |

**Example (L1-U1-M1 — enter the house):**
```
Learner picks: こんばんは (partial)
  → Mrs. Tanaka: 「こんばんは…どうぞ。」 (slight pause — she noticed)
  → Dialogue chain continues
  → Later in M2 (dinner), Mrs. Tanaka says: 「いらっしゃい！」 and waits
  → Learner now has a natural second chance to say おじゃまします
  → If they do → relationshipLevels[tanaka] +1 AND formalityPreference shifts polite
```

**Design rules:**
- Only ONE fail-forward per mission (or the scene collapses)
- Fail-forward always triggers a flagged recovery window in the next 1–2 missions
- If learner nails the recovery, emotional feedback acknowledges it: "Mrs. Tanaka gives you a small smile — she remembered."
- Critical errors (wrong verb, wrong particle breaking meaning) always retry, never fail-forward

**Signals to the learner:**
- Fail-forward is NEVER flagged as "wrong"
- A subtle moment of character awkwardness IS the feedback
- After the mission, the emotional feedback line might gently reference it: "You got there in the end."

---

### Layer 3 — Story Hook System (Retention)

Every level ends with a **hook** — a narrative promise that makes the learner want to return tomorrow.

**Three hook types, rotated across levels:**

| Type | What it does | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Cliffhanger** | Leaves a scene unfinished | "Yuki texts: 『あした、じかんある？』 You don't know how to answer yet…" |
| **Invitation tease** | Character offers something you can't accept yet | "Keita: 『こんど、カフェいこう！』 But first — you need to understand prices." |
| **Mystery drop** | New character or question appears | "You pass a bookshop. An older woman waves at you like she knows you. Who?" |

**Hook placement:**
- Shown AFTER the level badge, on the world map
- Includes the character's avatar + one-line tease in Japanese (with translation)
- Locked state: the next level's first mission card shows the hook as its subtitle

**Hook mapping per level (replaces generic "Next: ..." text):**
| Level end | Hook |
|-----------|------|
| L1 | "Mrs. Tanaka puts a notebook on your desk: 『あした、がっこうで がんばって！』 First real school day — tomorrow." |
| L2 | "Yuki waves as you leave: 『どようび、ひま？』 You don't know how to answer yet. But you will." |
| L3 | "Yuki points at a shop window: 『これ、なに？』 You realize — you can't name half the things you see." |
| L4 | "Yuki hands you a notebook with someone else's name on it: 『これ、だれの？』" |
| L5 | "You take a wrong turn walking home. An old man: 『どこへ いきますか？』" |
| L6 | "Mr. Tanaka asks: 『まいあさ、なにをしますか？』 Now you describe a whole day." |
| L7–L13 | Each hook teases the next location/character/grammar without showing it |
| L14 | "One year later. The plane home is tomorrow. You have one night left in Tokyo. Say goodbye properly." |

**Retention psychology:**
- Hooks are specific, not generic ("Next: verbs" becomes "The day you finally tell Yuki what you ate for breakfast")
- They name a character the learner already cares about
- They imply a specific capability the learner doesn't have yet
- They are delivered in Japanese (with translation), so the hook itself is a tiny learning moment

### Layer 4 — Challenge Missions

Each level has ONE optional Challenge Mission — harder, no scaffolding, mixed content, unlocks after the level checkpoint.

**Structure:**
```
Challenge Mission
├── Unlocks: after level checkpoint passes with 80%+
├── Scenario: an unexpected real-life situation using ALL level content
├── No vocab panel, no grammar hints, no retries (one attempt per question)
├── Pass threshold: 90%
├── Length: 15 mixed questions
└── Reward: Challenge Badge + unlock casual-register variant of level characters
```

**Examples:**
| Level | Challenge scenario |
|-------|-------------------|
| L1 | "You're locked out. Mrs. Tanaka is on the phone. Handle the whole evening in Japanese — from the doorstep to goodnight — without a single hint." |
| L2 | "Transfer student from Korea arrives. You have to introduce yourself AND explain who everyone else in class is — to them." |
| L3 | "The café is double-booked. You have to reschedule with BOTH Yuki and Keita, on different days, in one text thread." |

**Rewards beyond cosmetic:**
- Challenge Badge (separate from Level Badge)
- Unlocks "casual mode" for that level's characters (their relationshipLevel jumps to 3, they speak more casually from this point forward)
- Vocab mastered via challenge skips one SRS interval (rewarded with progress)
- Optional: unlocks a "behind the scenes" micro-scene (e.g., Yuki's perspective of your first day)

### Layer 5 — Speaking System (Future-Ready Integration Points)

Speaking is not implemented yet, but the mission structure must leave hooks for it. Defined now so future speaking tasks slot in without rewriting.

**Three speaking task types (to add to QuestionType union later):**

| Type | What it does | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| `pronunciation-repeat` | Learner hears a model, repeats, system scores closeness | Audio: おはようございます → Record → Match score |
| `dialogue-speak` | Learner SPEAKS their turn in a dialogue chain instead of typing | Yuki says なまえは？ → You speak: わたしは___です |
| `shadow-read` | Learner reads aloud with timing/accent guide | Display: いただきます with timing bar, learner speaks in rhythm |

**Integration rules:**
- Every dialogue chain already has a learner turn — speaking mode just changes input from text to voice
- Every vocabulary item already has an optional `audioKey` — speaking mode uses it as the model
- Missions gain an optional `speakingVariant: true` flag — same scenario, voice-first input
- At mission end, if speaking mode is active, emotional feedback references delivery: "Yuki grins — you even got the intonation right."

**When to introduce speaking:**
- Make speaking variant optional per level (toggle in settings)
- Auto-suggest it after the learner hits `confidenceLevel: high` on 3 consecutive levels
- Always available on review missions first (lower stakes)

**No new data model needed** — reuse existing audio keys, reuse dialogue chains, reuse mission structure. Speaking is an *input mode*, not a new mission type.

---

### Layer 6 — Dynamic Character Evolution

Characters are not static. The more the learner interacts with them, the more their speech, tone, and behavior change.

**Character evolution model:**

Each character has 4 relationship stages (tied to `relationshipLevels[characterId]`):

| Stage | 0 — Stranger | 1 — Acquaintance | 2 — Friend | 3 — Close |
|-------|--------------|------------------|------------|-----------|
| When | First meeting | After 3 missions | After 7 missions | After checkpoint + challenge |
| Speech | Formal, slow, clearly enunciated | Still formal but faster | Mix of casual + formal, jokes | Casual, nicknames, teasing |
| Honorifics | Full ~さん | ~さん | ~さん / ~くん / ~ちゃん | Drops honorifics |
| Reactions | Polite neutral | Warmer, more expressive | Physical gestures (hand on arm, laugh) | Inside jokes, callbacks |

**Per-character evolution:**

| Character | Stage 0 | Stage 3 |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Yuki | 「よろしくおねがいします。」 polite smile | 「ねー、きいてよ〜！」 grabs your arm |
| Tanaka-sensei | Firm, distant nod | Small personal comment: 「きょうは、つかれた？」 |
| Mrs. Tanaka | 「いらっしゃい」 formal host | 「おかえり〜、ごはん、たべた？」 family member |
| Keita | 「はじめまして。」 hands in pockets | 「おう！ひさしぶり！」 fist bump |
| Sora | Silent nods | Shares a book recommendation unprompted |

**How evolution surfaces:**
- Dialogue chains have stage-variant lines: Stage 0 Yuki says 「どこからきましたか」, Stage 3 Yuki says 「どこから来たの？」
- Emotional feedback references the current stage: Stage 0 = "Yuki smiled politely," Stage 3 = "Yuki rolled her eyes and laughed."
- Branching choices shift: at higher stages, the "correct" option leans casual; picking polite now becomes the partial (too stiff).
- Callback lines appear: at Stage 3, a character might reference a past mission — "れいぞうこ、おぼえてる？" (Remember the fridge incident?)

**Design rule:** Character evolution is the main long-term reward. Badges fade; a friendship arc sticks.

### Layer 7 — Layered Emotional Feedback

Upgrade the single `successMessage` into a **performance-aware feedback stack**.

**Current model (single line):**
```
successMessage: "You can greet a teacher correctly."
```

**Upgraded model (3 performance tiers):**
```
emotionalFeedback: {
  perfect: "Tanaka-sensei gives the firmest nod of approval. You nailed it."
  good:    "Tanaka-sensei nods. You got through it — and she noticed."
  struggle:"Tanaka-sensei waits patiently. You got there. It's day one — that's enough."
}
successMessage: "You can greet a teacher correctly in Japanese."
```

**Performance thresholds:**
| Tier | Trigger |
|------|--------|
| `perfect` | All tasks first try, no hints shown |
| `good` | 1–2 retries total, no hint overuse |
| `struggle` | 3+ retries OR heavy hint use |

**Writing rules:**
- All three tiers must preserve the same narrative truth (the learner DID succeed — tier only changes the FEEL)
- Never penalizing — struggle tier still celebrates, just more quietly
- Character-specific: Yuki's struggle tier is warm ("Yuki laughs — you'll get it"), Tanaka-sensei's struggle tier is stoic ("A quiet nod. That's a pass.")
- Always tie to the branching outcome the learner chose (referenced callback)

**Implementation hook:**
- Existing `successMessage` stays as the fallback
- New optional `emotionalFeedback` field on `JLearnStep`
- Engine picks tier based on `StudySessionAnswerRecord` accuracy + hint count

All eight layers feed ONE unified experience. This is the dependency map:

```
  └─────┬─────┘         └─────┬─────┘         └─────┬─────┘
```

##### Mission 1 — What time is it? (`L3-U2-M1`)
*(estimatedMinutes: 1 | new vocab: 8)*

**Scenario:** Yuki spots you between classes: 「ねえ、いまなんじ？」She forgot her watch. Tell her the time.
**Goal:** Say whole-hour times and understand AM/PM.

Grammar — embedded:
> Yuki asked: いまなんじ？ — "What time is it now?"
> Pattern: number + じ + です = "It's X o'clock."
> For AM say ごぜん, for PM say ごご. Put them BEFORE the number: ごぜんくじ = 9 AM.

Vocabulary (8 words):
| Japanese | Romaji | English |
|----------|--------|---------|
| 〜じ | ~ji | o'clock |
| なんじ | nanji | what time |
| ごぜん | gozen | AM |
| ごご | gogo | PM |
| いま | ima | now |
| とけい | tokei | clock, watch |
| あさ | asa | morning |
| よる | yoru | evening, night |

**Branching choice — it's 10 AM. Yuki asks いまなんじ？ What do you say?**
1. じゅうです
2. じゅうじです
3. ごぜんじゅうじです

**Outcomes:**
1. Yuki laughs: 「じゅう？ なにが十？」You said "ten" without the hour marker. *(Hint → retry)*
2. Yuki nods — works, but she already knew it was morning. You could be more specific. *(Partial)*
3. Yuki claps: 「かんぺき！」 → Dialogue chain unlocks.

Examples (3):
1. いまなんじですか。 — What time is it now?
2. ごじです。 — It's 5 o'clock.
3. ごごしちじです。 — It's 7 PM.

Tasks (6):
1. `sentence-build` — Build "It's 3": さんじです
2. `translation-en-jp` — "It's 9 AM." → ごぜんくじです
3. `multiple-choice` — What does ごぜん mean? → [**AM** / PM / now / evening]
4. `fill-in-blank` — いまなん___ですか。 → じ
5. `context-select` — It's 8 PM. Say: → [ごぜんはちじ / **ごごはちじ** / はちじあさ]
6. **Dialogue chain** (2 turns):
   ```
   [Yuki]: いまなんじ？
   [You]: ___                              → ごごさんじです。
   ```

## Guidelines for Scaling to N5 and N4 (JL4–JL21)

> Levels 1, 2, and 3 are now fully gold-standard (branching choices + emotional feedback + dialogue chains + recurring characters). The rules below govern ALL future levels. Every future mission MUST include: scenario, goal, grammar-embedded block, branching choice (3 options with Wrong/Partial/Correct outcomes), vocabulary, dialogue chain (2+ turns, scaling with level), emotional feedback, and successMessage.

---

### Non-Negotiable Mission Rules

| Rule | Requirement |
|------|------------|
| Scenario first | Write the real-life situation BEFORE writing any vocab or tasks |
| Grammar embedded | Show usage in an example FIRST, then explain in 1 plain-English sentence |
| Branching choice | Every M1, M2, M4 must have 3 options with distinct Wrong/Partial/Correct outcomes showing character reactions |
| Task ratio | ≥2 sentence-build, ≥1 dialogue-chain (2+ turns), ≥1 context-select, ≤1 multiple-choice per mission |
| Review missions | Same vocab, completely different scenario from M1/M2, no branching needed |
| Checkpoint purity | No vocab panels, no grammar hints, no scaffolding — pure production |
| Emotional feedback | Every mission ends with one line naming a feeling — never a score |
| Success messages | Name a real capability, never praise performance |
| Unit continuity | Last example/dialogue in each unit previews 1–2 words from the next unit |
| Recurring characters | Every level must use at least 2 returning characters from prior levels |

---

### Narrative Continuity Rules (N5 levels JL4–JL14)

The exchange student story continues through every level. Each level must:
- Reference a specific location on the Japan journey
- Have a unit-level mini-story
- Use at least one recurring character (Yuki, Keita, Tanaka family, Yamada-sensei)
- End with the learner in a slightly more capable place than they started

**Suggested narrative beats per N5 level:**

| Level | Story beat | New recurring element |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|
| JL4 — Demonstratives | Shopping with Yuki — "what's that?" | コンビニ clerk |
| JL5 — Possession | Lost notebook at school | New friend Keita |
| JL6 — Location | Getting lost in the neighborhood | Neighborhood elderly couple |
| JL7 — Verbs | Morning routine at the Tanaka home | Mrs. Tanaka |
| JL8 — Daily Routines | Your weekly schedule + Yuki's schedule | School timetable |
| JL9 — Adjectives | Describing the market food | Vendor character |
| JL10 — Past Tense | Telling Mrs. Tanaka about your day | Tanaka family dinner |
| JL11 — Te-form | Explaining a sequence of events | PE teacher |
| JL12 — Requests | Asking classmates for help with homework | Study group |
| JL13 — Reasons | Explaining why you're late | School front gate |
| JL14 — N5 Review | Full day in Japan — every situation reviewed | All characters appear |

---

### Dialogue Chain Scaling (complexity grows per level)

| Level range | Chain length | Complexity |
|-------------|-------------|------------|
| JL1–JL3 | 2–3 turns | Simple exchanges (greeting, response) |
| JL4–JL7 | 3–4 turns | Include a question + answer + follow-up |
| JL8–JL10 | 4–5 turns | Full conversational arc (request → response → clarification) |
| JL11–JL14 | 5–6 turns | Multi-topic conversation + grammar stacking |
| JL15+ (N4) | 6+ turns | Natural back-and-forth with subordinate clauses |

---

### SRS Integration per Level

Each new level adds its vocab to the review pool. Rules:
- Previous level vocab always eligible for warm-up review
- Weak items (errorCount > 2) from any prior level can appear in any review mission
- At N5 midpoint (JL7), introduce a "mixed review session" type that draws randomly from JL1–JL6
- At N5 completion (JL14), trigger a "Full N5 Review Sprint" — 50-question session across all levels

---

### N4 Design Principles (JL15–JL21)

N4 builds on N5 grammar, not replaces it. Design rules:
- Every N4 mission must use at least 1 grammar pattern from N5 alongside the new N4 pattern
- Dialogue chains must now STACK grammar (e.g., conditional + past tense in one exchange)
- Scenarios become more complex: misunderstandings, clarifications, multi-step requests
- Review missions explicitly contrast N5 and N4 forms (e.g., ます-form vs te-form usage)

**N4 Narrative:** By JL15, the learner is in their second semester. They're more fluent, taking on harder challenges — a part-time job at a café, a school festival, a day trip with the host family.
